# ADR-0050: AMD FSR 3.1, run by the engine

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-14
- **Amends:** ADR-0049 (FSR 3.1 is kept as an option, though it does not beat the engine's upscaler), ADR-0028 (FSR 3.1, not FSR 2)

## Context

Step 9.16 of the roadmap. ADR-0049 set a rule: FSR 3.1 is added if the engine's upscaler at 67% exceeds 1.15 times native resolution's FLIP, and kept if it beats the engine's. The mirror room failed the rule (1.34 times still, 1.43 panning).

Before FSR was built, the mirror room was broken down, to see whether the gap was the upscaler's or the lighting's, which works in render pixels and which FSR would get the same (`UpscalingTest.DISABLED_BreakDownTheMirrorRoom`, ratios of 67% over 100%, still / panning):

| Mirror room | Ratio |
|---|---|
| As measured in ADR-0049 | 1.38 / 1.41 |
| Without its glass | 1.32 / 1.38 |
| Without reflections | 1.26 / 1.27 |
| Direct light only | 1.87 / 1.83 |
| Nothing lit, its surfaces glowing: no noise | 1.60 / 1.54 |

The gap grows as the lighting's noise goes: it is the upscaler's. The noise adds the same error at both scales and hid it. FSR was built.

## Decision

**AMD's passes, the engine's resources.** AMD's FidelityFX SDK 1.1.4 (MIT) holds FSR 3.1's upscaler as HLSL headers, which read and write through callbacks, and a host library with its own Vulkan backend: its own descriptors, barriers and shader compiler. The headers are vendored unchanged in `third_party/fidelityfx-fsr3`; the host library is not. `shaders/Fsr3.slangh` implements the callbacks over the engine's bindless arrays: each resource is found by an index, in one buffer written each frame with FSR's constants. Slang compiles AMD's HLSL as it does the engine's shaders. `Fsr3Upscaler` does what AMD's context does: it creates FSR's textures, fills its constants, clears what AMD clears, and adds its seven passes to the render graph, which places the barriers and validates them. Each is one of AMD's pass files, with the engine's entry point: prepare inputs, luma pyramid, shading change pyramid, shading change, prepare reactivity, luma instability, accumulate. FSR's own sharpening and its debug view are left out; the output pass sharpens FSR's image as it does the engine's.

**FSR's options.** HDR color, motion vectors at the render resolution and without jitter, reverse-Z with a far plane, no auto-exposure: the color is exposed already. Its jitter is the engine's, the other way around. Its textures are at the output's size, its maximum render size, and the frame is in their top-left corner, as ADR-0049 draws it. The transparency and composition mask is empty; the particles' reactive mask is FSR's.

**The RHI** gains three formats FSR needs: `R16Float`, `R32Float`, and `R32Uint`, which compute shaders update atomically.

**An option, not the default.** `renderer.upscaler` is `engine` (the default) or `fsr`. A change starts the new upscaler's history over.

## Measurements

RTX 3090, 320 × 180 output, 67%, as ADR-0049 measures (`UpscalingTest.DISABLED_CompareWithFsr`); FLIP over native resolution's, still / panning:

| | Engine | FSR 3.1 |
|---|---|---|
| Edges alone | 1.56 / 1.22 | 2.27 / 1.68 |
| Dark corridor | 1.14 / 1.10 | 1.23 / 1.16 |
| Mirror room | 1.38 / 1.44 | 1.40 / 1.46 |
| Mirror room, nothing lit | 1.60 / 1.54 | 1.62 / 1.58 |

Flicker between still frames, in the dark corridor: 0.0039 native, 0.0062 the engine's at 67%, 0.0024 FSR's. In every scene, FSR flickers two to three times less than the engine's upscaler, and less than native resolution.

FSR does not beat the engine's upscaler by the rule's measure: its error is 1 to 8% higher in lit scenes, and much higher on edges alone. It is steadier. With its jitter the other way, edges alone go from 0.0063 to 0.0085: the integration's mistakes show in the measure. In the factory at 1440p and 67%, FSR costs 0.78 ms, the engine's resolve 0.32 ms; screenshots of the floor show the same detail.

The owner kept FSR as an option: steadiness is worth having in dark scenes where shimmer shows, and the rule's measure does not weigh it.

## Consequences

- Two upscalers to keep working. The engine's stays the default; both have tests.
- AMD's headers are updated by hand, by copying a newer release's into `third_party/fidelityfx-fsr3` and running the tests. FSR 3.1 is AMD's last upscaler with its source: SDK 2.0 ships FSR as signed binaries, and FSR 4 needs AMD's newest GPUs.
- The shaders compile on any platform Slang does: Phase 14's Linux build needs nothing more.
- FSR's sharpening, debug view and frame generation are not used.

## Tests

- **`UpscalingTest.FsrAtTwoThirdsStaysNearTheOutputsResolution`:** FSR at 67% in the dark corridor, within 1.3 times native still and 1.25 panning, flickering less than native; edges alone below 0.0075, which a jitter the wrong way exceeds.
- **`RendererTest.UpscalesWithFsr`:** FSR at a scale that changes every frame, and changes of upscaler both ways, with validation clean.
- **Measurements, not tests:** `UpscalingTest.DISABLED_BreakDownTheMirrorRoom` and `UpscalingTest.DISABLED_CompareWithFsr`.
